# Why Socratic works the way it does

Every rule in [`SKILL.md`](../skills/socratic/SKILL.md) traces back to the Socratic method itself, to learning science, or to what went wrong when people built LLM tutors. This page maps each rule to that evidence.

## The method, in one paragraph

Socrates did not lecture. In the *elenchus* the learner states a belief, agrees to a few premises, and then sees for themselves that the premises contradict the belief ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socratic_method)). The resulting *aporia*, "neither knows nor fancies that he knows", is progress, because "now he will wish to remedy his ignorance" ([*Meno* 84a–c](https://www.uvm.edu/~jbailly/courses/Plato/menoPassage80-86withNotes.html)). The teacher acts as a *midwife* (maieutics): the idea comes from the learner. Modern readers keep the core and drop the flaws. In the *Meno*, the boy never gets to ask a question of his own, many steps are yes/no assent, and the ending turns into a lecture ([Weiler Gur Arye 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10780-025-09545-2)).

## Rule → evidence

| Rule in the skill | Why |
|---|---|
| **Elicit, diagnose, ask the contradicting question, have them restate the belief** | This is the elenchus, rebuilt for code. Most novice bugs come from a false belief about how the program runs ([Qian & Lehman 2017](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3077618)). Good Socratic debugging walks the learner to a fact about the program that contradicts that belief ([Al-Hossami & Bunescu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00371)). |
| **Predict, then run** | PRIMM puts *Predict* before *Run*: committing to a prediction turns the mismatch into the lesson ([Sentance et al. 2019](https://primmportal.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/teaching-computer-programming-with-primm-a-sociocultural-perspective.pdf)). Novices need to trace code before they can write it ([Lister et al. 2004](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1041624.1041673), [Xie et al. 2019](https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/papers/Xie2019IntroCSTheoryOfInstruction.pdf)). |
| **Focus, don't funnel** | Funneling questions march the student to the teacher's answer, so the teacher does the thinking. Focusing questions build on the student's own reasoning ([Herbel-Eisenmann & Breyfogle 2005](https://resources.corwin.com/sites/default/files/5.0_overview_of_questioning_and_discourse_0.pdf)), and they correlate with better instruction and outcomes ([Alic, Demszky et al.](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/Funneling_vs_Focusing_Questions.pdf), [Demszky et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.130)). Paul & Elder: "Base each of your questions on the answer given" ([Thinker's Guide](https://www.criticalthinking.org/files/SocraticQuestioning2006.pdf)). |
| **Hint ladder, up after failure, down after success** | *Contingent tutoring*: more specific help after a failure, less after a success ([Wood & Middleton](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475296000230), [scaffolding](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/932126)). AutoTutor uses the same steps: pump, hint, prompt, assertion ([Graesser et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED586836.pdf)). A deployed tutor used an eight-rung ladder ([Pisan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292)). |
| **After the answer, they explain it back or apply it** | Even a bottom-out hint helps, but only if the student processes it ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)). Prompting self-explanation improves understanding ([Chi et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1803_3)). |
| **Answer conceptual questions directly; show progress every 2–3 turns; treat "idk" as frustration** | In a two-year field study, students largely stopped using Khanmigo once it refused answers and only asked questions ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-khanmigo-math-ai-tutor/), [NBER w35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)). Real students route around a chatbot's teaching to reach their own goals ([Neagu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15766)). CodeAid answers conceptual questions but never hands over the solution code ([Kazemitabaar et al., CHI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11314)). Tutors also manage frustration ([Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/932126)). |
| **Before you send: does the question contain its answer?** | LLM tutors give answers away often ([MRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09416v2), [MathDial](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.372/), [TreeInstruct](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.553/)). Leaks come in stages: the solution itself, then naming the exact bug, then over-explaining general facts. A prompt alone does not hold the line under pressure, which is why the skill keeps an explicit rung and a pre-send check ([Pisan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292)). |
| **Verify before you steer; judge by evidence, not phrasing** | LLMs show "uncanny confidence when giving an incorrect answer or when marking a correct reply as incorrect" ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)). A good tutor must recognise correct answers as well as wrong ones ([SocraticLM, NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/9bae399d1f34b8650351c1bd3692aeae-Abstract-Conference.html)). An execution result outranks the model's opinion ([Pisan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292)). |
| **Kind but firm under pushback** | Models give way when the student cites authority or applies social pressure ("please don't tell me I'm wrong") ([Kasneci & Kasneci 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14604)). Mentioning an answer shifts the model's accuracy toward it ([Arvin 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10297)). |
| **Adapt to expertise** | Guidance that helps novices hurts experts: the expertise reversal effect ([Kalyuga 2007](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/EDC608/Kalyuga2007_Article_ExpertiseReversalEffectAndItsI.pdf)). Worked examples that fade, with self-explanation prompts, ease the move to solving problems alone ([Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill 2003](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ678596)). |
| **Plan 2–5 steps; one part at a time; never repeat a failed question** | A system prompt alone could not keep multi-part problems in order ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)). Explicit prerequisite structure beats model scale for long tutoring sessions ([Tio et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11744)). Repeated and irrelevant questions are tagged failure modes ([Socratic Debugging benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03210)). |
| **Fact vs judgment questions; no guess-what-I'm-thinking** | Paul & Elder separate one-answer questions from reasoned-judgment ones ([Art of Asking Essential Questions](https://www.criticalthinking.org/store/products/-the-art-of-asking-essential-questions-based-on-critical-thinking-concepts-and-socratic-principles/168)). Questions whose answer the teacher already knows reward guessing over reasoning (Mehan, [ISLS](https://repository.isls.org/bitstream/1/3141/1/11-18.pdf)). |
| **Praise the move, not the person; call hard things hard** | Person-level praise is the least effective feedback ([Hattie & Timperley 2007](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/575/Hattie_Timperly_2007.pdf)). "Wise feedback", high standards plus confidence the student can meet them, increases revision ([Yeager et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23937186/)). Expert tutors stress how hard the problem is ([Lepper & Woolverton, INSPIRE](https://www.eoas.ubc.ca/research/cwsei/resources/INSPIRE-Guidelines.pdf)). |
| **Learner states the recap; end with a transfer question** | Retrieval beats re-reading ([Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2006/2006_Roediger_Karpicke_PsychSci.pdf), [Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)). Transfer "rarely happens by accident" and needs bridging ([Perkins & Salomon](https://www1.udel.edu/dssep/transfer/teaching_for_transfer.htm)). |
| **Say why you ask; never feign ignorance; offer choices** | Learners need transparency and control over an AI tutor (CodeAid design guidance). Expert tutors offer choices and go along with student requests (INSPIRE). |
| **They write the 5–10 lines that carry the decision** | Claude Code's Learning output style hands the human the code at decision points and writes the boilerplate itself ([README](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/blob/main/plugins/learning-output-style/README.md)). |
| **Levels** | *lite* is "generate, then comprehend", one of the high-scoring patterns when developers learned with AI ([Anthropic 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)). *ultra* is productive failure: attempting before instruction improves conceptual understanding and transfer ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.3102/00346543211019105)). |

## The headline results

- **Unguarded AI hurts learning; guardrails remove the harm.** In a field RCT with about 1,000 high-school students, an answer-giving GPT raised practice scores but lowered unassisted exam scores. A hint-giving, answer-guarded tutor removed that harm ([Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)).
- **A well-designed AI tutor can beat active learning.** It gave one step at a time, never the full solution at once, and asked for an attempt first ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)).
- **Struggle is the point.** "Cognitive effort—and even getting painfully stuck—is likely important for fostering mastery" ([Anthropic 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)).
- **But a tutor that only asks gets abandoned.** That is why the skill answers conceptual questions, demands visible progress, and always honours "just tell me".

## Known limits

A prompt can't run an outside judge. Studies that enforced answer-withholding or step order did it in the platform, not the prompt ([Pisan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292), [Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)). The skill uses the cheap substitutes: an explicit hint rung and a check before every message. Expect occasional leaks under heavy pressure.
